# Rights and reuse

Open 1650 records rights at the same granularity as its source data. Historic
text, source scores, new metadata, and platform software are distinct layers.

## Historic Psalm text

The seventeenth-century Scottish Psalter wording is public-domain material and
is marked `PDM-1.0` in corpus records. The
[Free Church of Scotland](https://freechurch.org/praise-resources/) states that
Scottish Psalter Psalm texts may be reproduced without copyright concerns.

## New transcription and metadata

Project-created transcription, collation, correction, annotation, tune-event
metadata, and lyric underlay are intended for dedication under `CC0-1.0`.
Contributors must confirm that they created or are authorized to dedicate their
work. Attribution and source lineage remain recorded as historical evidence
even though reuse is not conditioned on attribution.

Before a public editorial release, maintainers should add the full CC0 legal
instrument and obtain explicit contributor affirmations.

## Source images and modern formatting

Public-domain words or music do not make every scan, photograph, typeface, page
design, or modern editorial apparatus public domain. Open 1650 links to source
materials and records their terms; source images and PDFs are not bundled by
default.

The Free Church of Scotland's words-only PDF, PlainText archive, and combined
tune-recommendation edition are received-text and catalog references. The
Psalm text is treated under the publisher's reproduction statement; their
contemporary page design and editorial apparatus are not treated as
project-owned material and the source files are not redistributed. The
University of Michigan EEBO-TCP keyboarded text for A76561 is CC0, while EEBO
page images have separate conditions.

## Tune, harmony, and setting data

The 27 encoded melodies and source harmonizations are historic public-domain
material:

- Twenty-five tunes follow named score and MIDI records in the
  [Christian Classics Ethereal Hymnary](https://www.ccel.org/cceh/): ST. PETER,
  CRIMOND, ABBEY, WIGTOWN, DUNDEE (FRENCH), ST. FLAVIAN, ST. ANNE, WINCHESTER
  OLD, SELMA, BANGOR, CAITHNESS, COLESHILL, CULROSS, KILMARNOCK, MARTYRDOM,
  TALLIS' ORDINAL, YORK, TALLIS' CANON, WARRINGTON, OLD HUNDRED THIRTY-FOURTH,
  BISHOPGARTH, OLD HUNDRED TWENTY-FOURTH, CROFT'S HUNDRED THIRTY-SIXTH,
  ST. CECILIA, and ST. JOHN (PARISH CHOIR). The archive's
  [rights statement](https://www.ccel.org/cceh/cceh_cpr.htm) places the
  collection in the public domain and permits copying, modification,
  distribution, publication, and performance.
- OLD HUNDREDTH follows Mutopia's
  [public-domain edition](https://www.mutopiaproject.org/cgibin/piece-info.cgi?id=194).
- AULD LANG SYNE follows Philip Rice's public-domain
  [CPDL edition](https://www.cpdl.org/wiki/index.php/Auld_lang_syne_(Traditional)).
  Only its four-line common-meter verse section is encoded; the refrain is not
  copied into the tune record.

Tune records mark historic composition, arrangement, and source-event
transcription as `PDM-1.0`. New MIDI-event metadata, proportional timing
normalization, the 163 Psalm-to-tune underlays, syllable alignment, and
generated MIDI files are intended for `CC0-1.0`. Algorithmic metrical fitting
is project metadata: it remains provisional and does not change the
public-domain diplomatic Psalm layer.

No third-party recording or sampled instrument is bundled. All four playback
parts are synthesized locally from the checked-in event data.
